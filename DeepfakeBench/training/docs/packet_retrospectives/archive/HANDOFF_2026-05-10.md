## The user's framing constraint (load-bearing — do not deprioritize this)

Their *individual* identities will not appear in production. **But identities with similar IQ profiles, color signatures, lighting conditions, or capture-pipeline quirks WILL appear in production, intermittently and unpredictably.**

So when an evaluation lens "drops chronic-6" (the F4 substrate filter, `analysis/substrate_cleaning_eval_2026-05-05/`), it is not a free pass. **F4 numbers are aspirational under an IQ-gate deployment policy** — they describe model capability *if* a pre-gate filters out the chronic-6-equivalent population. They do *not* describe what production-grade FPR looks like on a population that includes one Roy_D-equivalent user.

Under this constraint:
- A model that wins on F4 v2 + dominates HDTF clean + partially generalizes on HDTF teams + catastrophically false-flags one specific identity with unusual color saturation → **is not robustly deployable as-is**, because that color signature could appear in production.
- A model that's slightly weaker on F4 viso + handles every identity reasonably → **may be more robust** for a real-world rollout.

**This is the core question for your assessment**: which model do we ship? The strict-F0 candidate (P8A or T3_SLOT1_step1500), or the F4-frontier candidate (T3_SLOT1_step2500)? And if neither is deployable as-is, what's the cheapest experiment to break the trade-off?
