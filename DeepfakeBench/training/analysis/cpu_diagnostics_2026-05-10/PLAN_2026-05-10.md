## TL;DR

**The encoder is the structural bottleneck.** All 7 ckpts measured (P8A, E2B, T3 step1500, T3 step2500, MCLIOEXB, P18T, P18C) have **forgery and shortcut signals BOTH extractable at >0.9 AUC from L11** features. None achieves `forgery_AUC > max_shortcut_AUC` at any layer. The HEAD chooses what to use, but the ENCODER's L11 features are entangled — and **FT-from-P8A interventions cannot escape this** (proven by MCLIOEXB face_scale_jitter NOT weakening face_size_high; P18 GRL NOT weakening is_chronic_6).

**The user's intuition is empirically validated**: FT-based recipes have an absolute ceiling on encoder invariance (~0.025-0.031 inv_mean). To break it, we need either:
- **Path β** (recommended): from-scratch B16 + multi-axis adversarial GRL during training
- **Path δ** (complementary): forgery-localization auxiliary loss

Before any GPU spend, **3 CPU pre-tests (~1 day)** validate the from-scratch+GRL thesis. If pre-tests show inv_mean > 0.10 is achievable for some recipe, GPU launch is justified.

## What the diagnostics showed

### Finding 1 — Encoder L11 invariance is bounded across ALL ckpts tested

(Source: `outputs/forgery_signal_atlas_extended.csv`, 14 ckpts × 5 layers × 6 signals)

| ckpt | recipe | forgery (real_v_fake) | is_chronic_6 | inv_max | inv_mean |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MCLIOEXB | P14 face_scale_jitter@0.5 winner | 0.979 | 0.899 | -0.015 | **0.031** |
| P8A | FT chain (production anchor) | 0.976 | 0.909 | -0.019 | 0.027 |
| T3_step1500 | FT-from-P8A + drop high-Lap reals | 0.992 | 0.945 | -0.005 | 0.024 |
| T3_step2500 | same lever, longer training | 0.991 | 0.956 | -0.008 | 0.021 |
| P18T | FT + 12-class method-conditional GRL | 0.981 | 0.934 | -0.017 | 0.021 |
| P18C | P18 control | 0.981 | 0.935 | -0.018 | 0.018 |
| E2B | from-scratch+CE (deployed) | 0.995 | 0.975 | -0.004 | 0.020 |

**inv_mean range: 0.018-0.031**. The absolute spread is 0.013 — within probe noise. Across 7 distinct training recipes (FT chain, FT+jitter, FT+drop-list, FT+GRL, scratch+CE), the encoder's L11 invariance is essentially constant.

**The invariance gap to the goal**: target inv_mean > 0.10 (forgery cleanly above shortcuts); current best 0.031. Need ~3× lift on a metric that hasn't moved across 7 recipes.

### Finding 2 — face_scale_jitter does NOT structurally weaken the face-size shortcut

(MCLIOEXB face_size_high AUC at L11 = **0.949** = P8A's 0.949)

The P14 winner mclioexb (face_scale_jitter@0.5 alone) was the strongest single-lever data-axis result before T3. It does NOT change face_size shortcut extractability at the encoder. **Whatever mclioexb did, it operated at the head level** (calibration), not at the encoder level (representation).

**Implication**: T4 = Slot 1 + face_scale_jitter@0.5 (which I previously endorsed) would NOT be a structural fix for the color/face/IQ shortcuts. It might still help via head calibration but cannot break the encoder bottleneck.

### Finding 3 — P18 multi-axis GRL did NOT bite the encoder

(P18T is_chronic_6 at L11 = 0.934 vs P8A 0.909 — actually slightly higher leakage)

P18 trained with a 12-class method-conditional GRL auxiliary. Empirically, **P18T/P18C do not have lower shortcut AUCs than P8A at L11** — the GRL was head-level-defensive, not encoder-shaping.

**Implication**: a fresh multi-axis-GRL packet would need to do something P18 didn't — e.g., GRL classifiers attached directly to L11 features (not at head output), higher λ, or different axes (is_dor / is_chronic / IQ-quartile rather than method-cluster).

### Finding 4 — viso is structurally blurrier than deeplive

(viso fakes lap_var p50 ~30-75, deeplive fakes lap_var p50 ~240; missed-viso are even blurrier)

Viso is intrinsically a low-IQ method. The "high-Lap = real" tendency in the model directly competes with catching viso fakes. T3 step2500's viso lift to 65% recall came from breaking that tendency — but at the cost of shortcut leakage on chronic identities.

**Implication**: To fix viso recall WITHOUT shortcut leakage, the model needs to attend to LOCAL forgery cues (swap-region artifacts) instead of GLOBAL IQ statistics. This argues for forgery-localization auxiliary loss (Path δ).

### Finding 5 — P8A's broad-cohort fragility is mostly gate-shielded

(9/11 P8A unique catastrophic-FPs on sessions with 0-5% medium-gate retention)

The P8A "11 unique catastrophic FPs" finding from Job B looks alarming but disaggregates: 7 are on Q__s6 (min_dim=95 — tiny crops, gate filters 100%), 2 on Md_noyn_Sharker__s15 (95% gate filtered), 2 on Test_Cam__s73 (28% strict-gate retained). **Post-strict-gate P8A risk = ~2 frames out of 4564 broad cohort**. step2500 has ~13-20 frames surviving strict gate.

**Implication for deployment**: P8A is even more deployment-safe than the macro Job B numbers suggested. step2500 has REAL post-gate fragility on chronic-6 sessions.

### Finding 6 — T3 step1500 inherits P8A's L11 subspace

(P8A↔step1500 cos at L11 = 0.552; E2B↔step1500 cos at L11 = -0.178)

T3 step1500 rotated P8A's L11 representation by ~57° (cos 0.55) but did NOT converge with E2B (cos -0.18 — orthogonal). T3 carved out a new region of L11 representation space, partially preserving P8A's invariance properties.

**Implication**: T3 step1500 is a real alternative deployment candidate (separate from the encoder-invariance question). It has the cleanest broad-cohort behavior (0 unique catastrophic FPs, lowest pre-gate FPR 8.43%, never outlier-high on disputed reals) of any candidate. This is the deployment-decision side of the analysis.

## The plan — three CPU pre-tests, then one GPU experiment

### Phase 2A — CPU pre-tests (~1 day, $0)

These three pre-tests determine which Path is GPU-ready.

#### Pre-test 1 — Multi-axis GRL on frozen L11 features (Path α/β feasibility)

**Question**: can multi-axis-GRL classifiers attached to L11 features force inv_mean > 0.10 in any training regime?

**Method**:
- Use cached L11 features for {P8A, E2B, MCLIOEXB} on the 800-frame triptych
- Train a small head: linear classifier real/fake + 5 GRL-attached classifiers for {is_dor, is_chronic_6, lap_var_q, min_dim_q, color_a_dev_q}
- Sweep λ_GRL in {0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0}
- Measure inv_mean = forgery_AUC - mean(shortcut_AUCs) on the trained head's representation

**Outcome decision tree**:
- If achievable inv_mean > 0.10 with reasonable λ → **Path α/β GPU-justified.** Encoder needs shaping, not just head calibration.
- If achievable inv_mean stays at 0.03 even with λ=5.0 → multi-axis-GRL approach is structurally limited at this representation. Pivot to Path δ.
- If achievable inv_mean drops forgery_AUC below 0.7 → over-pressuring breaks forgery; need different mechanism.

**Cost**: ~3 hours. All sklearn / PyTorch on cached features.

#### Pre-test 2 — L6 head prototype (read forgery from earlier layer)

**Question**: would a head reading from L6 (where forgery saturates first, before shortcut amplification at L11) produce a more invariant model?

**Method**:
- Use cached L6 features for P8A on 800-frame triptych
- Train a 2-layer MLP head: L6 features → real/fake
- Compare to the model's actual prediction at L11
- Measure inv_mean on L6 representation
- Score `teams_real_all_dev` (or sample) using L6+head; compare to P8A's L11+head behavior

**Outcome**:
- If L6+head matches P8A's forgery_AUC AND has inv_mean > 0.10 → architectural variant: deploy a head reading from L6, not L11. Could be retroactively applied to P8A weights with a head-only retrain.
- If L6+head loses forgery_AUC significantly → L11 amplification is doing useful work; can't shortcut to L6.

**Cost**: ~2 hours.

#### Pre-test 3 — Grad-CAM saliency analysis on P8A vs T3 step1500

**Question**: does either ckpt already attend to the swap region on v2 fakes? This determines Path δ's room.

**Method**:
- Generate Grad-CAM saliency masks for P8A and T3_step1500 on a sample of v2 viso + deeplive fakes
- Get swap-region masks from v2 pair manifests (where available) or face-detection (face crop ≈ swap region for v2)
- Compute mask-vs-swap-region IoU per frame
- Compare distributions

**Outcome**:
- If P8A IoU > 0.7 already → forgery-localization is already in P8A's mechanism; Path δ adds little.
- If P8A IoU < 0.3 → Path δ has substantial room. Forgery-localization auxiliary loss could meaningfully change attention.
- If T3 IoU > P8A IoU → T3 implicitly learned more local attention. Useful insight for next packet.

**Cost**: ~3 hours. Requires Grad-CAM on cached images, swap-mask derivation.

### Phase 2B — One GPU experiment based on Phase 2A outcomes

After the pre-tests, ONE of these GPU experiments is justified by sharp thesis + close criteria:

#### Option β1 — From-scratch B16 + multi-axis GRL (predicted to be the indicated path)

**Thesis**: a fresh B16 trained end-to-end with multi-axis adversarial GRL classifiers attached at L11 will achieve `inv_mean > 0.10` (a 3× lift over current best) at the encoder, while preserving forgery_AUC > 0.95.

**Recipe**:
- Architecture: B16-DataComp-XL backbone, fresh init from CLIP weights (not from P8A)
- Loss: CE on real/fake at head + 5× GRL-attached binary classifiers at L11 features for {is_dor, is_chronic_6, lap_var quartile≥p75, min_dim quartile≥p75, color_a_dev quartile≥p75}
- λ_GRL: tuned in pre-test 1
- Training data: same as P8A (no data-axis lever — to isolate the architectural intervention)
- LR scheduler: warmup → cosine
- Total steps: ~5000
- Periodic ckpts: every 500 steps

**Close criteria** (must hit ALL to be promotion candidate):
1. `inv_mean > 0.05` at L11 on the 800-frame triptych (50%+ improvement over current best 0.031)
2. `forgery_AUC > 0.97` at L11 (within 0.02 of P8A)
3. `is_chronic_6 AUC < 0.85` at L11 (vs P8A 0.909 — measurable encoder-level chronic invariance)
4. F4 deeplive recall ≥ 90% at FPR=10% (matches P8A baseline)
5. F4 viso recall ≥ 65% at FPR=10% (within 2pp of step1500's 73%)
6. ≤ 6/92 may6 false-flag rate (matches step1500's may6 floor)

**Cost**: ~$100-150 GPU on Vertex (full from-scratch training run).

**Pivot conditions**:
- If close criteria 1-3 hit but 4-6 miss → encoder works, head/data needs work; iterate as Phase 3.
- If close criteria 1-3 miss → multi-axis-GRL approach doesn't structurally work at this scale; pivot to Path δ.

#### Option δ1 — Forgery-localization auxiliary loss (alternative if pre-test 1 says GRL won't work)

**Thesis**: forcing Grad-CAM (or attention-rollout) masks to align with swap regions during training will produce a model that uses LOCAL forgery cues, with structurally lower shortcut leakage.

**Recipe**:
- Same B16 from-scratch
- Auxiliary loss: KL(attention_mask, swap_region_mask) for all training frames where swap region is known (~70% of training data)
- Standard CE on real/fake at head
- Otherwise identical to β1

**Close criteria**:
1. Mean attention-vs-swap-region IoU > 0.7 (vs current ~?-? per pre-test 3)
2. F4 viso recall > 80% at FPR=10% (proves local-forgery attention helps the structurally-blurry method)
3. is_chronic_6 AUC < 0.85 at L11 (encoder-level invariance)
4. ≤ 6/92 may6 false-flag rate

**Cost**: ~$120-180 GPU + infra investment (mask derivation pipeline).

#### Option γ1 — Curriculum from-scratch (defer unless β1 and δ1 both fail)

Curriculum has the most degrees of freedom and the loosest thesis. Save for if β1 + δ1 both miss their close criteria. The user's intuition supports it but needs more concrete stage criteria first.
