## Headline

**Refuted as proposed**, with a secondary finding that supersedes the original
question: at any matched lockbox-FPR operating point, **per-mode τ (whether
substrate=clip_capture_mode or substrate=IQ-feature-cluster) is WORSE OR
EQUAL to a naive global τ** calibrated directly on lockbox. The May 5 "24.5pp
recall lift" was an apples-to-oranges comparison (different operating points,
not different policies at the same point).

## Why the May 5 "24.5pp lift" was misleading

The May 5 `USAGE.md` reported:

> per-mode dev-calibrated achieves teams_fake_lockbox recall **78.59% at
> 16.91% lockbox real FPR** vs **54.12% recall at 4.30% lockbox FPR for the
> single global tau** — a 24.5pp recall lift at 4× the FPR

This compared per-mode-oracle (16.91% FPR) to single-global-τ at a
**different operating point** (4.30% FPR). The two policies landed at
different lockbox FPRs because dev-calibration translates differently into
lockbox FPR depending on which τ rule is used.

At the same lockbox FPR (16.91%), naive global τ achieves ~85.2% recall —
6.6pp MORE than per-mode-oracle's 78.6%. The "lift" was a calibration
artifact, not a policy-quality lift.

## Why IQ-substrate per-mode is even worse

- K-means cluster definitions are fit on dev frames; dev/lockbox within-cluster
  distributions differ (and differ MORE for IQ buckets than for
  clip_capture_mode, which is a more semantic discriminator).
- Per-mode τs are dev-calibrated; their lockbox FPR realization is noisier
  per-bucket than per-clip_capture_mode.
- At k=8, oracle gets close to clip_capture_mode oracle on absolute recall
  (72% vs 78.6%) but lands at HIGHER lockbox FPR (26% vs 17%) — still loses
  at apples-to-apples.

## Why Phase 2 (R/G/B channel means) was cancelled

Phase 2 was conditional on Phase 1 producing a small lift that more features
could amplify. Phase 1's actual failure mode is **dev→lockbox per-bucket
calibration transfer**, not feature insufficiency. More features → smaller
clusters → noisier per-bucket τ → WORSE transfer. Phase 2 would not help.

## Why Phase 3 (Slot A v2) was cancelled

Phase 3 was conditional on Phase 1 passing. Phase 1 refuted the approach on
P8A. While Slot A v2 might behave differently, the underlying mechanism
(dev/lockbox per-bucket distribution mismatch) is ckpt-independent. The
expected outcome on Slot A v2 is the same refutation.

## OPINIONS — what this means for the program

1. **The "per-substrate τ" strategic option needs to be downgraded.** It was
   listed as a candidate in multiple handoffs (HANDOFF.md, MORNING_DECISION_TREE).
   The apples-to-apples comparison shows it doesn't beat naive global τ
   calibrated on a lockbox-shaped real set. The exception (clip_capture_mode
   at tau_strict, +9.2pp) is one cell at a very tight operating point and
   needs independent validation before being treated as a real lever.

2. **The deeper insight**: the binding deployment problem isn't "the model
   uses transport-signature" — that's a known property that we keep targeting.
   It's "dev/lockbox distributions differ within every substrate definition
   we've tried." Per-mode anything is fighting the wrong axis.

3. **What WOULD likely help (untested)**: calibrate τ on a real-frame set
   that is **drawn from the same distribution as production** — i.e., the
   `multi-account capture sweep` referenced in
   `analysis/teams_account_natural_experiment_2026-05-19/§6.1`. If lockbox
   IS a faithful production sample, then lockbox-calibrated naive global τ
   IS the deployment recommendation. The 75.5% recall at 10% lockbox FPR
   (P8A) is the deployable curve. Slot A v2's equivalent curve hasn't been
   computed; if it dominates P8A's, that's the ship.

4. **The 2026-05-19 natural experiment** showed per-Teams-account
   transport-flip is real. Tonight refuted one fix (training-time codec aug
   on top of anchor). Today refutes another (per-frame IQ-feature τ
   calibration). The remaining strategic option from the original list is
   **per-account metadata from Teams' SDK at deployment** (if accessible) —
   that is a discrete-class signal, not the noisy continuous IQ proxy I
   tested today.
