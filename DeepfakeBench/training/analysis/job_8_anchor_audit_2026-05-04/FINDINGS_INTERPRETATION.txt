## OPINION (lens 1) — substrate-invariance is not a single-number metric

**OPINION, may be wrong**: the three Lens 1 summary metrics (Δmean,
Δp50, KS-stat) order the candidates differently. Δmean ranks E2B_3200
most-invariant; Δp50 ranks E3_6600 most-invariant; KS-stat ranks
E3_6600 most-invariant; Δp90 ranks E2B_3200 most-invariant.

Reading these together: E3_6600 has near-zero median shift (Δp50 = 2.5e-4
on reals, 1.5e-4 on fakes) but the largest mean shift (Δmean = 0.066
on reals). One reading is that E3_6600's score distribution is concentrated
near both extremes (0 for reals, 1 for fakes) so the median barely moves
between substrates while the mean — pulled by a thicker tail — does. The
shape of the distribution (spike-near-0 + heavy tail) makes the median
metric flatter than any of the others.

P8A's pattern (Δmean 0.038, Δp50 0.009, Δp90 0.42) suggests its real-class
tail moves significantly between substrates while the bulk does not.
Whether that's "more substrate-invariant than E3_6600" or "less" depends
on which moment you weight.

**Caveat — this is the reading that could be wrong**: Job 7 asserted P8A
is substrate-invariant on the existing head and that a frozen-feature
retrain destroys that invariance. The Job 7 measurement (per the dispatch
prompt) was apparently real_dev mean ≈ real_lockbox mean. By that
measurement E2B_3200 wins (Δmean = 0.016), not P8A. If Job 7's framing
of P8A's invariance has been over-stated, this audit's Δmean column
would imply E2B_3200 is the more substrate-stable real-class predictor
— but only on that one lens, and only on the mean.

## OPINION (lens 2) — calibration lift size depends on starting point

**OPINION, may be wrong**: at FPR=2%, E3_6600 shows enormous lifts
(+0.60 to +0.64) on three of four suites, while at FPR=10% its lifts
collapse to <+0.16. The mechanism: at FPR=2%, τ_dev_cal ≈ 0.9941 sits
above E3_6600's fake p50 on three suites — so dev-cal recall is
mechanically near zero, and any τ relaxation toward lockbox-cal reveals
a large fraction of fakes that are already-detected representationally.

This means "Lens 2 lift" measures TWO things together:
1. How much more separable fakes are when you trust lockbox negatives;
2. How extreme the dev quantile's τ ends up being.

E2B_3200's small lifts at FPR=10% (≤+0.075) likely reflect its lower
τ_dev_cal (0.51 vs P8A 0.71 vs E3 0.85) — its dev-cal recall is already
high, so there is less left to recover.

P8A's mid-range lift at FPR=10% on teams_fake_lockbox (+0.214) reproduces
Job 7's "free 21pp" finding within rounding (0.214 vs reported 0.21).

**The reading that could be wrong**: characterizing lift as "deployment
recall recoverable via per-substrate τ" assumes the lockbox-calibrated τ
is a deployable threshold. But lockbox-calibrated τ uses the lockbox itself
as the calibration set — in production this requires a separate held-out
real-substrate sample with known capture-mode distribution. If no such
sample exists at deployment time, the lift is unrealizable.

## OPINION (lens 3) — the IQ-shortcut signal is heavily confounded with class imbalance

**OPINION, may be wrong**: P8A's 0.7909 CV accuracy — lift of +5.99pp
over its majority baseline (0.7309) — is the only one of the three CV
results that meaningfully exceeds its baseline. E2B_3200's 0.9145 sits
DOWN −0.18pp from its 0.9164 baseline; E3_6600's 0.8673 sits UP only
+0.55pp from its 0.8618 baseline.

This is consistent with two simultaneous truths:
(a) E2B_3200 and E3_6600 catch very few viso fakes at FPR=10% (46 / 76
    of 550); the prediction problem becomes "guess the majority class";
(b) when n_caught is small, the L1 logreg has fewer minority-class
    examples to learn from, biasing CV accuracy toward the trivial baseline.

The CV AUC is more informative under imbalance: P8A 0.7474, E2B_3200
0.8665, E3_6600 0.7651. By that read, **E2B_3200's catch decisions look
MORE explainable by IQ features alone** than the other two — opposite
of what the CV-accuracy column suggests. (Job 4's original framing —
"viso CV acc 0.72-0.76 → IQ shortcut" — used CV accuracy on a single
ckpt where baseline was less degenerate; extending it to E2B/E3 needs
the AUC view, not the accuracy view.)

Top-1 features differ across ckpts (saturation_mean / sobel_edge_mean /
luma_p10), and signs differ. This rules out a clean "all three ckpts use
the same IQ shortcut feature" reading.

**The reading that could be wrong**: AUC under heavy imbalance is also
unstable across folds (E2B std 0.050; P8A std 0.034). The "E2B is more
IQ-explainable" inference relies on a difference of ~0.10 AUC under
those error bars, which is at most a 2σ effect.

## OPINION (lens 4) — gap-AUC-vs-recall is dominated by AUC level, not by ckpt

**OPINION, may be wrong**: the largest gaps cluster on viso (48-60 pp
across all three ckpts), and gap is reasonably correlated with (1 − AUC):
when AUC is 0.68-0.75 (viso for all three), gap is ~50 pp; when AUC is
0.95+ (deeplive for E2B/E3, teams_fake_lockbox for E3), gap is ≤5 pp.

This means Lens 4 mostly tells us which (ckpt × suite) pairs are
saturated and which are not. It does not cleanly differentiate the three
ckpts within a suite.

**Suite-specific picture**:
- viso — all three ckpts have huge gaps (48-60 pp). Operational recall
  at FPR=10% never exceeds 27% on viso.
- deeplive — E2B and E3 are saturated (gap < 5 pp); P8A has 44pp gap.
- teams_fake_lockbox — E3 is saturated (gap 2.2 pp); P8A has 36pp gap.
- teams_fake_dev — all three are partially saturated (gap 12-21 pp).

A reading: P8A has "the most threshold-tuning headroom" on three of four
suites; E3 has the least (only viso is unsaturated); E2B is in between.
**But "headroom" is an asymmetric coin — large gap also means current
calibration is leaving recall on the table OR that the model's
probability scale is just poorly calibrated.** This metric does not say
whether more aggressive τ would actually deliver the implied recall
without inflating FPR.

## OPINION (cross-lens) — the candidates dominate different lenses

**OPINION, may be wrong**: each ckpt is best on a different summary lens:

- E2B_3200: smallest Δmean on real / smallest Δmean on fake / highest
  IQ-shortcut CV AUC.
- E3_6600: smallest Δp50 / smallest KS-stat / largest L2 lift at FPR=2%
  / smallest gap on teams_fake_lockbox / highest L4 AUC on
  teams_fake_lockbox.
- P8A: largest L2 lift at FPR=10% on teams_fake_lockbox / largest IQ-shortcut
  margin over baseline on viso / largest gap on three of four suites
  (which is "most headroom" or "least calibrated," depending on your
  read).

Pulling these together: there is no single ckpt that wins ≥3 of 4 lenses
on any consistent summary metric. The decision matrix in
`anchor_decision_matrix.csv` rewards different ckpts depending on which
column the user weights.

**The reading that could be wrong**: framing the audit as "each ckpt
wins a different lens" is itself an opinion that obscures one possible
ground truth, namely that no candidate is operationally adequate on
viso (recall@FPR=10% caps at 27% across all three ckpts; viso ceiling
unbroken across 13+ R13 packets per project memory). If the binding
constraint is viso recall, none of the three is a working anchor by
that constraint, and lens-by-lens comparison among them may not be the
right framing at all.

## OPINION (lens with highest cross-ckpt variance)

**OPINION, may be wrong**: the metric with the largest cross-ckpt range
relative to its own scale appears to be **L2 lift at FPR=10% on
teams_fake_lockbox**: P8A +0.214, E2B_3200 +0.075, E3_6600 +0.049 — a
2.9× to 4.4× ratio. **L2 lift at FPR=2% on deeplive** is also very wide:
P8A +0.231, E2B_3200 +0.273, E3_6600 +0.637.

Lens 1's KS-stat range (0.317-0.382) is narrower in relative terms.
Lens 3's CV AUC range (0.747-0.867) is narrower still. Lens 4's AUCs
range across ckpts within the same suite by 0.07-0.12 AUC points,
which is moderate.

A reader who weights "the most cross-ckpt-discriminating lens" would
weight Lens 2. A reader who is suspicious of Lens 2 (because it depends
on availability of a deployable per-substrate calibration sample) might
weight Lens 1's KS-stat instead.
