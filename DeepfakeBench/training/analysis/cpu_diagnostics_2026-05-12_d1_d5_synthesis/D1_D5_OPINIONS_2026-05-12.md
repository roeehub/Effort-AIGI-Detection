## 1. The synthesis — "the deeper story" in one paragraph

The forgery signal is already in raw CLIP B16: D2 shows CLIP-frozen L11 features give a 5-fold CV probe AUC = 1.000 within the chronic-6 sub-cohort with no FT at all. Standard FT does not add the forgery signal — FT modifies (a) how the signal is projected through the head and (b) pulls the encoder's chronic-6 separation sub-space slightly toward color-axis IQ alignment (D2: chronic-6 IQ-PC1 angle drifts from CLIP-frozen's 83.68° to FT'd ckpts' 74.95°-78.54°, a 9°-13° drop below the 87.5° random-orthogonality baseline; smallest per-axis angles consistently on `color_a_dev` / `saturation_mean`). FT-induced behavior splits along two distinct shortcut modes: **P8A learns identity-cluster memorization** (D5: per-identity output R²=0.22 with mean-IQ; D1: large residuals after IQ-control on specific known identities, e.g. `real_dor` residual logit = −2.33; per-identity coefficients SIGN-FLIP across chronic-6 — `saturation_mean` β=−6.15 on Roy_D vs +3.33 on dor_shkedi). **T5C step3500's head over-projects the encoder's slightly-IQ-aligned chronic-6 sub-space** (D5: identity-output R²=0.60 even though the encoder direction is 75° from IQ PC1; this means T5C's IQ-correlation lives in the head's amplification, not in the encoder's direction). The encoder of every FT'd ckpt is substrate-specific in its separation direction (D3: DEV→LOCKBOX probe AUC 0.594-0.747 across 4 ckpts versus in-sample CV 0.999 — the encoder retains separating signal within each substrate but a different direction in each). Output-level per-IQ-quartile FPR transfers reasonably for P8A (D4: r=+0.72 dev↔lockbox quartile alignment) but is broken for T3 (D4: r=−0.03); T3's data-filter intervention introduced substrate-specific cohort responses rather than a substrate-agnostic axis rule. The path to a single-model deployment candidate has two viable variants: **(a) preserve CLIP-frozen's natural IQ-orthogonality from step 0** via B16-scratch + Fourier-aug bands 8-13 + continuous-axis-GRL + T3 keep-list data filter [Slot 2], or **(b) actively repair an FT base's chronic-6 IQ-angle drift** via continuous-axis-GRL on T5C step3500 [Slot 1]. P8A-output distillation and P8A-L11 feature anchor levers are off the table — they propagate P8A's identity-cluster memorization (which doesn't generalize to new identities) and P8A's per-identity-sign-flipped color-axis rules (which don't compose into a generalizable principle).

## 2. Per-diagnostic interpretation (read pass-2 against the FACTS docs)

### 2.1 D1 — P8A IQ-shortcut alignment within chronic-6

**Numbers**: chronic-6 joint R²=0.35 with dominant `saturation_mean` β=+4.27; healthy joint R²=0.16 with `saturation_mean` β=−1.08 (opposite sign, ~4× smaller magnitude); per-identity R² range 0.18-0.76 with `saturation_mean` β = −6.15 on Roy_D vs +3.33 on dor_shkedi (sign flip).

**My reading**: P8A's chronic-6 score is partly IQ-explained at the frame level (R²=0.35 is non-trivial), but the IQ rule is NOT axis-coherent — it flips sign across chronic identities. This is direct evidence against the "P8A learned a generalizable IQ rule" framing. The per-identity sign-flip means distilling P8A's output on these identities transfers a CONSTELLATION OF IDENTITY-SPECIFIC RULES, not a generalizable axis principle. A new model trained to mimic P8A on these 5 identities would inherit the sign-flipped rules and fail to generalize them to a 6th identity with similar IQ but different identity-cluster.

**Alternative interpretation worth considering**: the per-identity sign flips might be an artifact of multicollinearity (sum of univariate r² = 0.425 > joint R² = 0.350). The per-identity regressions have n in [372, 863] frames; multicollinearity between IQ axes within a small identity cohort could produce unstable β signs. CIs were not reported per-identity. A critic should check whether the sign flips persist under stricter multicollinearity regularization (e.g., ridge regression).

### 2.2 D5 — Per-identity FPR by mean-IQ across 3 ckpts

**Numbers**: per-identity joint R² P8A=0.22, T3_S1_step1500=0.49, T5C_step3500=0.60. Sign-of-residual cleanly partitions chronic-6 from non-chronic (5/5 chronic above the IQ-predicted line; 5/6 non-chronic below) across all 3 ckpts. Top-residual identities (`real_dor`, `Cam_Test`, `PC_Generator`) recur across ckpts with stable sign.

**My reading**: P8A's per-identity score is mostly NOT IQ-explained → P8A is doing identity-cluster discrimination not IQ-based scoring. T5C step3500's per-identity score IS mostly IQ-explained → T5C is doing IQ-based scoring. The FT progression P8A → T5C didn't remove the shortcut; it SWAPPED P8A's identity-memorization shortcut for T5C's axis-shortcut. Reconciliation with D2 (where T5C's encoder direction is 75° from IQ-PC1 = mostly orthogonal): the IQ-projection in T5C lives at the HEAD, not the encoder. T5C's head amplifies whatever IQ-alignment exists in the encoder's slightly-aligned chronic-6 sub-space.

**Alternative interpretation worth considering**: with n=11 identities and 6 predictors (dof=4), the R² gap 0.22 vs 0.60 might be inflated by overfit on the larger model's training trajectory. The R² estimate at dof=4 has high variance. The chronic_6 residual sign partition (5/5 vs 5/6) is stronger evidence than the per-ckpt R² values. A critic should weight the partition heavier than the R² differential.

### 2.3 D4 — Per-IQ-quartile FPR alignment dev↔lockbox

**Numbers**: aggregate alignment correlation across 6 axes: P8A r=+0.72 |Δ|=0.026; T5C_step3500 r=+0.51 |Δ|=0.050; T3_S1_step1500 r=−0.03 |Δ|=0.046. Largest single delta cells are on `color_a_dev` Q4 for T5C (dev 18.5% / lockbox 4.2%) and T3 (dev 14.3% / lockbox 0.0%). Aggregate at dev-calibrated 5% τ: lockbox FPR is LOWER than dev for all 3 ckpts.

**My reading**: P8A's output behavior is substrate-stable along IQ axes (telling P8A a frame's IQ profile predicts its score regardless of substrate). T3_SLOT1's behavior is NOT substrate-stable — its per-IQ-quartile FPR pattern in dev is essentially uncorrelated with lockbox. This is a direct strike against T3 as a base for Slot 3 composition: composing with T5C's classifier capacity would inherit T3's substrate-instability. The "lockbox is easier than dev at dev-cal τ" finding contradicts the common framing of "lockbox is the hard substrate" — at simple FPR-cal, lockbox is actually easier; the contract scorecard's `lockbox_real_fpr` tiebreak is measuring per-quartile residual structure, not overall difficulty.

**Alternative interpretation worth considering**: T3's r=−0.03 might be sample-size artifact. Per-axis Pearson r on 4 quartile cells is high-variance; lockbox-side per-quartile n drops to 14-32 in some cells. The aggregate per-ckpt r averaged across 6 axes is more stable but still subject to the same noise. A critic should weight the per-axis worst-cell-delta (T3 color_a_dev Q4 |Δ|=0.143) more than the aggregate r.

### 2.4 D3 — Cross-substrate probe transfer

**Numbers**: DEV→LOCKBOX AUC 0.594-0.747 across 4 ckpts; LOCKBOX→DEV AUC 0.825-0.951; in-sample CV AUC ≥ 0.999 in every cell. Substrate-agnostic criterion (both directions > 0.95): **0 of 4 ckpts pass**. Asymmetry LOCKBOX→DEV > DEV→LOCKBOX is +0.08 (T5C) to +0.30 (P8A). T5C step3500 has the LEAST asymmetry; P8A has the MOST.

**My reading**: This is the biggest reframe of the session. The previous A2-ext finding ("encoder retains separation, lockbox AUC=1.000") was correct in-sample but hid that the encoder retains DIFFERENT separations per substrate. The dev-lockbox gap is an ENCODER problem (the separation direction itself is substrate-specific), not just a head problem. Head-retrain on substrate-diverse data CAN'T bridge a 30pp encoder-direction gap. The structural fix is upstream — either start from scratch with anti-shortcut training (Slot 2), or actively repair the encoder direction during FT (Slot 1).

**Alternative interpretation worth considering**: the lockbox slice is only n=87 frames (small). Tier 2 escalation to a larger sample was not run. The 95% CIs are wide enough that the DEV→LOCKBOX AUC point estimates could shift ±0.05. A critic should check whether Tier 2 escalation should be run before accepting D3's encoder-problem framing as load-bearing.

### 2.5 D2 — Encoder separation-direction decomposition

**Numbers**: probe AUC ≥ 0.9969 on every cell; CLIP-frozen AUC=1.000 on chronic-6 with no FT. Angle to IQ-PC1 (deg) on chronic-6: CLIP_FROZEN 83.68; P8A 78.54; E2B 74.95; T5C_step3500 75.19; T3_S1_step1500 77.09. Random-orthogonality baseline in 512-d ≈ 87.5°. Smallest per-axis angles consistently on `color_a_dev` / `saturation_mean` for all FT'd ckpts on chronic-6.

**My reading**: The forgery signal is in CLIP. FT does not "teach" it; FT modifies its projection. On non-chronic features, no ckpt's separation direction is materially non-random with IQ. On chronic-6, every FT'd ckpt pulls the separation direction 9°-13° closer to IQ-PC1 than CLIP-frozen does. This is small enough that it doesn't dominate (the angle stays > 75° = mostly orthogonal), but large enough to be measured. The drift is specifically color-axis-aligned. This is the mechanism for the chronic-cohort FPR: FT learns to use the chronic-6 cohort's color-axis IQ profile as a (weak) fake predictor, even though the encoder's separation is mostly orthogonal to IQ in aggregate.

**Alternative interpretation worth considering**: angles in 512-d space are noisy; the 87.5° random baseline assumes uniformly-random unit vectors, which the trained probe weight vectors are not. The empirical baseline (e.g., angle between two real/fake probes from independent random subsets of the same cohort) was not computed. A critic should ask whether the 9°-13° below-baseline deviation is large compared to the empirical resampling variance.

### 3.1 Slot 2 — B16-scratch + Fourier-aug bands 8-13 + continuous-axis-GRL + T3 keep-list filter (HIGHEST priority)

- CLIP-frozen L11 features give chronic-6 AUC=1.000 with no FT (D2 §2). The forgery signal is in raw CLIP.
- FT pulls the chronic-6 encoder direction toward color-axis IQ-alignment (D2 §3, 9-13° angle drop).

**Mechanism**: scratch from CLIP B16 (DataComp-XL); apply continuous-axis-GRL on 6 IQ axes from step 0 (force the encoder's output to be IQ-decoupled); Fourier-aug bands 8-13 randomly perturbs the shortcut-carrying high-frequency amplitude bands at training time; T3 keep-list filter at the data level removes the high-Lap teams reals that ground the IQ-shortcut signal.

- Post-training chronic-6 IQ-PC1 angle ≥ 82° (matches or beats CLIP-frozen's 83.68° — the bundle preserved natural orthogonality)
- AND lockbox_real_fpr ≤ 0.025 at FPR-cal τ
- AND dev_fake_macro_recall ≥ 0.50

**Cost**: ~$40-50 (scratch training ~8-12h on A100). Single slot.

**Seed**: 9802.

### 3.2 Slot 1 — Continuous-axis-GRL on T5C step3500 base (HIGH priority)

- AND lockbox_fake_recall ≥ 0.55

**Cost**: ~$30-40 Vertex. Single slot.

## 5. What I might still be wrong about (the load-bearing uncertainties)

A critic reading this doc should weight these heavily.

1. **CLIP-frozen AUC=1.000 on chronic-6 may be sample-size inflated**. n=282 with 41 fakes → 5-fold CV folds have ~8 fakes per fold. The 1.000 estimate has high variance. A larger sample (e.g., extend chronic-6 to lockbox cohort with the same identities) would test robustness. If the AUC is actually ~0.95 at scale, the "forgery signal is in raw CLIP" framing weakens.

2. **The 87.5° random-orthogonality baseline is theoretical, not empirical**. In practice, trained LR-probe coefficients are correlated structure (not uniform random vectors). The empirical baseline (e.g., angle between probes trained on independent random subsets of the same data) might be 85°, not 87.5°. If so, the FT'd ckpts' 75-79° angles are less dramatically below baseline.

3. **D3's small lockbox slice (n=87) gives wide CIs (±0.05-0.10 on AUC)**. The point estimate P8A DEV→LOCKBOX 0.594 might be 0.55 or 0.65 at scale. Tier 2 escalation was the right call to defer in the brief but should be run before committing $40 GPU to Slot 4.

4. **D5's per-identity OLS has dof=4**. Per-axis β CIs are uninformative individually. The R² differential P8A 0.22 vs T5C 0.60 is robust at the aggregate level (it's hard for an overfit model to be at 0.22), but per-axis attributions are noisy.

5. **The "P8A is identity-memorizing" claim is based on residual structure (D5 §3.3)**, not on direct evidence of training-data overlap. The recurring top-residual identities (`real_dor`, `Cam_Test`, `PC_Generator`) might be in P8A's training set (testable via train manifest grep) OR they might just have features that the model handles cleanly for independent reasons. Memory `project_clean_teams_same_identity` says clean and teams buckets share identities, supporting the train-overlap interpretation, but a grep would close this.

6. **The "FT pulls chronic-6 angle toward color axes" finding is correlational, not causal**. FT might be doing many things; the chronic-6 angle drift might be a side-effect, not the mechanism for chronic-cohort FPR. A counterfactual test (e.g., FT with anti-color-axis regularization, measure whether angle drift is prevented and whether chronic FPR drops) would establish causality. This is essentially what Slot 1 tests.

7. **Slot 2 (B16-scratch + bundle) assumes the bundle works**. PD (`packets/PD.md`) showed corr-penalty can shift shortcut to untargeted axes. Continuous-axis-GRL on 6 axes might not be exhaustive — the encoder could shift to a 7th axis we haven't measured (skin_frac, edge_mag, contrast_l). The bundle is a compound experiment; its individual components have mixed records.

8. **The "lockbox is easier than dev at dev-cal τ" finding (D4)** contradicts the contract-scorecard reading and the user's hands-on experience that lockbox is the hard case. A critic should investigate whether D4's framing is consistent with deployment-relevant behavior, or whether it's an artifact of how FPR-cal τ is set.
