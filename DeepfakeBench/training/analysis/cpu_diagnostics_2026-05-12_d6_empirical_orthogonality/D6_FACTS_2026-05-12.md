## 1. Method

For each ckpt × sub_cohort × bootstrap b ∈ [1, B]:

1. Stratify-shuffle the cohort rows by label; assign half A, half B (disjoint, 50/50).
2. Train `sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression(C=1.0, max_iter=2000, n_jobs=1, solver="lbfgs")` on each half independently; extract `.coef_[0]` and normalize to unit length.
3. **rf-direction stability**: `angle_rf_b = arccos(|<w_rf_A, w_rf_B>|) · 180/π`. Distribution across B bootstraps gives noise floor.
4. **IQ-PC1-direction stability**: for each half, fit `LinearRegression` per IQ axis (6 axes), stack into 6×768, take first right-singular-vector as that half's IQ-PC1; normalize. Compute angle between the two halves' IQ-PC1 vectors.
5. **D2 replicate**: for each half independently, compute the angle between its rf-direction and its IQ-PC1 (this is the D2 §3 measurement done on half the data). Report distribution of the half-A / half-B values pooled across bootstraps.
6. Random-orthogonality baseline in 768-d: `arccos(sqrt(1/768)) ≈ 87.93°`.

All operations on cached features; no GCS; CPU only.

## 7. Caveats (load-bearing)

- **Feature dimension is 768-d** (the L11 cache stores pre-projection features). D2's `run_d2.py` reads the same cache so D2's reported angles are also in 768-d space, despite D2's docstring saying "512-d post-proj". The 87.93° random-orthogonality baseline for 768-d (vs D2's quoted 87.5° for 512-d) is the right reference; the difference is small enough that interpretation isn't materially affected.
- **D2's single-split angles are biased HIGH** relative to bootstrap means (z-scores +0.32 to +3.16 across cells; +2.12 to +3.16 on the chronic_6 cells). This is consistent with the bias from training on 100% of data vs 50% — more data → tighter probe → slightly different direction. The bootstrap MEAN is the "typical split outcome" estimator.
- **Bootstrap split is stratified by label**. Within chronic_6, 41 fakes split into ~20+21. Each LR-probe in the loop is trained on ~140 frames (half of 282). The probe AUC convergence at this scale is high (D2 reported chronic_6 in-sample AUC=1.0); the angle estimates are stable.
- **The IQ-PC1 stability cells in §3 are large (55°-75°)** — meaning the IQ-PC1 direction itself is noisy under bootstrap. This is because PC1 of 6 axis regressions only captures ~70% of axis variance; the remaining 30% rotates substantially between halves. The angle-to-IQ-PC1 measurement is therefore double-noisy (rf-direction noise + IQ-PC1 noise).
- **B=50 gives ~7σ standard error in std estimates** (std/√(2(B-1)) ≈ std/9.9), so the std columns in §2-§4 are reliable to ~±10% relative. Z-scores in §5 inherit this precision.
