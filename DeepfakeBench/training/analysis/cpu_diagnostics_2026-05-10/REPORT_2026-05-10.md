## 3. The 4 robustness diagnostics

The diagnostics were designed to expose behavior the macro metrics hide: per-axis-bin FPR variance, catastrophic-tail width, and disagreement structure across the 4 ckpts on the same frames.

### 3.1 Job A — Multi-axis robustness matrix

**Method**: for each ckpt, calibrate τ to give exactly 5% FPR on the overall real cohort (`teams_real_all_dev`, n=4564, 1898 with IQ-atlas data). Then bin reals + pooled fakes by IQ axis quartile (6 axes) and measure per-bin FPR + recall.

**Headline — each ckpt has a distinctive fragility axis**:

| ckpt | worst axis-bin | FPR there | recall there | fragility character |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| P8A | min_dim Q1 (lowest resolution) | **10.4%** | 31.2% | low-resolution reals over-fire |
| E2B | lap_var Q1 (blurriest) | **13.1%** | 34.4% | blurry reals over-fire |
| T3_S1_step1500 | color_a_dev Q4 (warmest) | **14.3%** | 66.7% | warm-color reals over-fire |
| T3_S1_step2500 | color_a_dev Q4 (warmest) | **20.4%** | 48.7% | same axis, twice as bad |

Mean FPR spread across all axis-bins:
- P8A: 7.3% (most decoupled per-axis)
- E2B: 8.7%
- step1500: 11.4%
- step2500: 15.3% (most axis-coupled)

P8A's strength on this metric is real but cuts both ways: it has the most decoupled per-axis behavior at calibrated 5% but ALSO has a literal **0% recall blind spot on lap_var Q4** (sharpest fakes). T3 ckpts don't have such hard zeros (min recall 8-9%).

### 3.2 Job B — Catastrophic-tail comparison

**Method**: count of reals from `teams_real_all_dev` (n=4564) scoring above various thresholds, regardless of calibration.

**Headline — step1500 has the narrowest tail; step2500 has the widest**:

| ckpt | reals > 0.5 | reals > 0.9 |
|---|---:|---:|
| **T3_S1_step1500** | **9.03%** | 5.30% |
| E2B | 10.08% | 4.52% |
| P8A | 12.20% | 6.53% |
| T3_S1_step2500 | **19.96%** | 8.30% |

**Unique catastrophic FPs** (real frames where one ckpt scores > 0.9 AND the other 3 ckpts all score < 0.5):

| ckpt | unique catastrophic-FP count |
|---|---:|
| **T3_S1_step1500** | **0** |
| E2B | 2 |
| P8A | 11 |
| T3_S1_step2500 | 60 |

Step1500 has ZERO unique catastrophic-FP frames — every frame it false-flags is also false-flagged by at least one other ckpt. P8A has 11 unique catastrophic-FP frames that NO other ckpt false-flags — these are the empirical signature of "P8A feels fragile." Step2500 has 60 such frames, mostly in the warm-color axis.

**Catastrophic-miss on fakes** (fake frames scoring < 0.3):

| Suite | P8A | E2B | step1500 | step2500 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| teams_fake_all_dev | 20.0% | 17.0% | 14.1% | **4.4%** |

Two findings here:
- **E2B has 86.5% catastrophic-miss on viso fakes** — confirms my earlier deployment-suspicion. Whatever ckpt ships, E2B should be retired.
- **P8A has 37% catastrophic-miss on deeplive fakes** — P8A's blind spot. Step1500 cuts this to 10.5%; step2500 to 0%.

**Trade-offs**:
- 17pp worse than P8A on HDTF visomaster_enhanced_teams_dev (77.4% vs 94.6%)
- 6.5% over 0.5 rate on may6 (vs P8A's 0%) — small but measurable production-drift sensitivity

The choice between Finding 2 (P8A) and Finding 3 (step1500) depends on which substrate the production traffic resembles more:
- **may6-like production drift** → P8A (0% false-flag) wins
- **diverse broad-cohort distribution** → step1500 (zero unique catastrophic-FPs) wins

If shadow-deployment is feasible, run both on production traffic and compare downstream. Both are substantial improvements over E2B.

#### Finding 4 — DO NOT ship step2500 (now multi-front refuted)

The prior session's "ship step2500 with FPR-calibrated τ" recommendation is empirically refuted on multiple fronts:

- **may6 production-drift retest**: 77.2% false-flag rate at τ=0.5; 64.1% over 0.7; 43.5% over 0.9. WORSE than E2B (57.6% / 37.0% / 17.4%). Day-to-day drift +0.670 — the largest of any candidate.

- **Color-axis fragility not shielded by IQ gate**: strict gate AMPLIFIES warm-color FPR to 50%
- **Highest outlier-high rate**: 53% of disputed real frames have step2500 as the most-aggressive scorer

The argument "the IQ gate filters out the regression population" doesn't survive empirical contact, and the may6 retest is the most direct production-realistic test possible. **Step2500 has the worst day-to-day drift behavior of any candidate** on the empirically-recorded production-fragility population.

Step2500 retains value as:
- The highest macro fake recall (F4 viso 79.27%, F4 deeplive 100%) — research benchmark
- The basis for T4 packet design — proves the data-axis lever can lift fake recall

But it should not ship in production.

## 10. Bottom line

**For the user's stated question — "which model is the most robust to different conditions while keeping a high fake recall in all of these diversity?" — the answer depends on which substrate matters more, but neither answer is the currently-deployed model and neither is step2500.**

The four key findings:

1. **E2B (currently deployed) is the may6 false-flagger.** Memory `project_xinhe_may6_falseflag_2026-05-06` recorded production false-flagging at 0.83-0.93 — the may6 retest today shows that was E2B's behavior, not P8A's. E2B has 57.6% false-flag rate on those production frames, +0.459 day-to-day drift, plus 86.5% catastrophic-miss on viso fakes and 43.5% on the production-target HDTF attack class. **Switch from E2B is empirically supported on three independent fronts.**

2. **P8A handles may6 production-drift perfectly** — 0/92 frames over 0.5, zero day-to-day drift. But P8A also has 11 unique catastrophic-FP frames on the broad eval cohort that no other ckpt has. Both observations are real; they live on different frame populations.

3. **Step1500 is the broad-cohort macro winner** with zero unique catastrophic-FP frames + lowest false-flag tail + better F4/F0 fake recall than P8A. Close second to P8A on may6 (6.5% over 0.5 vs P8A's 0%). The trade-off vs P8A is 17pp on HDTF visomaster_enhanced_teams.

4. **Step2500 is empirically the WORST production-drift candidate.** 77.2% may6 false-flag rate at τ=0.5 (worse than E2B); +0.670 day-to-day drift (the largest of any candidate). The color-axis fragility is real, structural, and the IQ gate doesn't shield it. **Do not ship.**

**Recommended path forward**:

1. Switch deployment from E2B (urgent; multi-front empirically supported)
2. Ship P8A as the primary deployment candidate (revised after may6 finding) OR step1500 if broad-cohort robustness matters more than may6-style drift (ideally shadow-deploy both and decide downstream)
3. Hold step2500 as research artifact only
4. Run T4 = Slot 1 + face_scale_jitter@0.50 next (~$50-70) to test whether jitter resolves the color-axis fragility while preserving step2500's fake-recall lift
